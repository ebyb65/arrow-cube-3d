# Block Escape 3D: prototype design spec

Owner: Lena (design and QA). Status: PARKED by the CEO on 24 Nov 2026 after playing the prototype.

CEO feedback: rolling blocks over the edges was confusing, and the cube with gates on its edges looked complicated.
Lesson for the next game: the rules must read in one glance on a phone; a 3D hook that needs explaining is a cost, not a feature.
Not scheduled: by the ideas review, game #3 starts only after Arrow Cube reaches production.

## The one-line pitch
Slide coloured blocks around the outside of a cube and out through gates of the same colour.
It is the familiar colour-block "escape" puzzle, but the board wraps around all six faces.

## Rules (as built in the prototype)
- The board is the surface of a cube: six faces of N x N cells (N = 3 for levels 1-12, 4 later).
- Each block is 1-4 cells on one face (shapes: 1, 2, 3 in a line, L, 2x2) and has a colour.
- Gates are glowing coloured stretches on the cube's 12 edges, 1-3 cells wide.
- Swipe a block: it slides until it touches another block or the edge of its face.
- Edge with a gate of the block's colour that is at least as wide as the block: the block leaves the cube.
- A gate of another colour is a wall.
- Swipe toward a plain edge the block is already touching: the block rolls over onto the next face
  (like a train going round a corner) and keeps sliding there. One roll per swipe, so a block never
  circles the cube on its own.
- Win: every block has left the cube. Stars: 3 at par (the solver's fewest swipes), 2 within par + 50%, 1 otherwise.
- Undo is free and unlimited. Hint shows one good move (an arrow on the block). Restart is free.

Why these rules:
- Colour-to-gate matching is a rule players of this genre already know, so the tutorial is one sentence.
- Rolling over edges is the 3D hook: it is the only reason the cube matters, and it is what Arrow Cube
  does not have. In Arrow Cube a block flies out in a fixed direction; here a block can travel.
- "Stop at the edge, swipe again to roll" keeps every swipe predictable and avoids endless loops.

How it differs from Arrow Cube (studio rule: build in parallel, launch one at a time, and the two games must not compete):
Arrow Cube is "which block can leave now?" (order). Block Escape is "how do I route this block to its gate?" (paths round the cube).
Shared code: Three.js scene, cube rotation, raycast picking, Capacitor wrapper.

## Levels in the prototype
- Levels are generated by a script and every one is proven solvable: a solver finds the full solution
  (breadth-first search for the fewest swipes); par is that number.
- Ramp: L1 one block next to its gate; L2 needs a roll; L3 first other-colour wall;
  L4-7 four blocks, two colours; L8-12 six blocks, two or three colours; later levels use a 4 x 4 face.
- QA: every level was played to the win screen in headless Chromium by real swipes on the blocks
  (turning the cube toward the block first, as a player would) at 360x740 and 412x915, light and dark.

## Monetisation (not in the prototype)
- Rewarded video, always optional: an extra hint after the free ones, or "remove one block" on a hard level.
- Interstitials only between levels, never during a puzzle; not before level 6.
- No lives, no energy, no timers. Undo stays free.
- IAP later (remove ads) once Google Play Billing is set up studio-wide.

## Open questions for the CEO's decision
1. Is the roll-over-the-edge move fun or confusing on your phone? (It is the hook; if it confuses, the game is just a 2D board.)
2. Are the gates easy to tell apart from blocks?
3. Is turning the cube to find blocks on the back faces a pleasure or a chore?

## Known gaps (prototype only)
- Colour is the only cue: colour-blind players need symbols on blocks and gates before any release.
- No sound, no haptics, no coins, no daily puzzle, no ads.
- Generation of 4 x 4 levels is slow (the solver's search space grows fast); a real level set would be
  generated offline and hand-checked, 200+ levels before launch.
- A block that rolls can end up on a face the camera can't see; the hint turns the cube, but normal play does not.
